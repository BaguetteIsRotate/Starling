During the late stage of Alzheimer’s Disease, neurodegeneration becomes severe and widespread across the brain. Patients lose the ability to respond to their environment, carry on conversations, or control movement, requiring around-the-clock personal care. Communication becomes nearly impossible, though individuals may still occasionally utter words or make groaning, moaning, or grunting sounds. Physical abilities steadily decline, leading to trouble sitting, walking, and swallowing—which significantly increases the risk of food or liquid entering the lungs and causing aspiration pneumonia. Families often notice dramatic physical changes, including severe unintended weight loss, loss of bowel and bladder control, increased sleeping, and a total lack of awareness of recent experiences or surroundings.
