Alzheimer's disease is a neurodegenerative disease which accounts for about two-thirds of dementia cases. In 2022, it was the seventh leading cause of death in the United States. Caused by an accumulation of abnormal plaques and tangles of nerve fibers in the brain, Alzheimer's disease remains incurable. However, some palliative medications may provide symptom relief.  [1]

At the earliest stages of the disease, individuals are often asymptomatic, though a noticable drop in the quantity of tau proteins in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) – the fluid surrounding the brain – may be an early biomarker. [1]

The second stage of the disease is Mild Cognitive Imapirment (MCI). Patients may begin to experience impairments in memory or impairments in other areas such as decision-making and language. However, patients often continue to function independently, work, and socialize. The progression from this stage to the last stage is around 10% per annum. [1]

The last stage of the disease is Dementia. Patients experience incapacitating memory impairment, language changes, and decline in visuospatial abilities. [1]

Regular physical activity has been shown to benfit individuls with dementia by improving cognitive function. It especially benefits executive functioning, reducing the fall risk and increasing the patient's ability to do everyday tasks. Excercise has also been shown to reduce symptoms like depression. [1]

It has also been shown that cognitive activities alongside physical and social activities may provide benefits for patients. [2]
