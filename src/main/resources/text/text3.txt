The middle stage of Alzheimer’s Disease is typically the longest phase of the disease. During this time, the neurodegeneration spreads to regions of the brain that control language, carry out reasoning, and process sensory information. As a result, patients start forgetting long-term memories alongside more recent events. As time passes, other well-known clinical symptoms emerge. These include decreased personal hygiene and sundowning behaviors (heightened confusion, anxiety, and restlessness in the late afternoon and evening). Families also frequently report personality changes including the development of paranoia, delusions, and compulsive behaviors. This may be accompanied by a distinct change in social interactions often marked by withdrawal, mood swings, and an inability to recognize close friends or relatives. The patient may also experience increased confusion regarding time and place, which raises the risk of wandering. There may be notable changes in appetite as a result of spatial and sensory disorientation. The result is usually a loss of interest in food, or unintended weight loss.
